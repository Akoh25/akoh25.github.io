5 Power BI DAX Formulas Every Business Owner Should Know

Most business owners who use Power BI spend 80% of their time on charts and colours — and almost no time on the formulas that actually make those charts meaningful. DAX (Data Analysis Expressions) is what separates a pretty dashboard from one that actually answers your business questions.

Here are the five DAX formulas I use most frequently when building dashboards for SMEs, NGOs, and corporate clients in Nigeria.

1. CALCULATE — The Most Powerful Formula in Power BI

CALCULATE lets you change the filter context of any calculation. In plain English: it lets you answer questions like "what were total sales in Lagos only?" or "how many staff left in Q1 specifically?"

Example: Lagos Sales = CALCULATE([Total Sales], Region = "Lagos")

2. TOTALYTD — Track Year-to-Date Performance

TOTALYTD calculates a running total from the start of the year to any selected date. This is essential for any business comparing current performance against annual targets.

Example: YTD Revenue = TOTALYTD([Total Revenue], DateTable[Date])

3. SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR — Compare This Year to Last Year

This formula lets you pull the same time period from the previous year for comparison. Instead of manually finding last year's numbers, Power BI calculates them automatically whenever you change your date filter.

Example: Last Year Revenue = CALCULATE([Total Revenue], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(DateTable[Date]))

4. DIVIDE — Safe Division That Won't Break Your Dashboard

Regular division in DAX crashes your visual when the denominator is zero. DIVIDE handles this gracefully — returning a blank or a custom value instead of an error.

Example: Win Rate = DIVIDE([Deals Won], [Total Deals], 0)

5. RANKX — Rank Your Products, Staff, or Regions

RANKX ranks items in a table based on any measure. Use it to find your top-performing sales rep, best-selling product category, or highest-attrition department.

Example: Sales Rank = RANKX(ALL(Products[Name]), [Total Sales])

Why Your Excel Spreadsheet Is Lying to You (And How to Fix It)

Every week I speak to SME owners who tell me their business is fine — because their Excel says so. Then I open the file and find the same five problems that quietly distort the numbers most business owners rely on daily.

Here is what to look for — and how to fix each one.

1. Hardcoded Numbers Hiding in Formulas

When someone types =A2*0.075 instead of referencing a VAT rate cell, that 7.5% is invisible to anyone reviewing the file. When the rate changes, nobody updates it — and your totals are silently wrong for months.

Fix: Create a clearly labelled "Settings" sheet where all rates and constants live. Reference those cells in every formula.

2. Dates Stored as Text

This is the most common Excel error I encounter. The cell looks like a date but Excel treats it as text — so your SUMIFS, pivot tables, and date filters all produce wrong results or simply stop working.

Fix: Select the column, go to Data → Text to Columns → Finish. This forces Excel to recognise the values as real dates.

3. Merged Cells in Data Tables

Merged cells look neat but they silently break sorting, filtering, and any formula that tries to reference data in that area.

Fix: Never merge cells in a data table. Use "Centre Across Selection" (Format Cells → Alignment) for the visual effect without breaking the data.

4. No Data Validation on Input Cells

If anyone can type anything into your input cells, someone will eventually type "N/A", "nil", or leave a space — and your formulas will return errors or silently exclude that row from totals.

Fix: Use Data Validation (Data → Data Validation) to restrict inputs to numbers, dates, or a dropdown list of accepted values.

5. Totals That Don't Include All Rows

When someone inserts a new row at the bottom of a table, a SUM formula that ends at a fixed row reference will miss that new data entirely — and the total looks correct without being correct.

Fix: Convert your data range to an Excel Table (Ctrl + T). Table formulas expand automatically as new rows are added.

If your spreadsheet was built by someone else — or by you more than six months ago — run through this checklist before trusting any number in it.

How Nigerian SMEs Can Use Data to Make Better Business Decisions

Most Nigerian small business owners make decisions based on memory, intuition, and experience. And for a long time, that works. But as your business grows — more staff, more products, more locations — the same intuition that built the business starts to slow it down.

Data is not a luxury for large corporations. It is the fastest way for an SME to see what is actually happening in the business, not just what feels like it is happening.

The Three Questions Every SME Should Be Able to Answer Instantly

  • Which product or service makes me the most profit (not just the most revenue)?
  • Which customers, locations, or time periods drive the majority of my sales?
  • Where is money leaving my business without me noticing?

If you cannot answer these three questions in under two minutes, you are running your business without a dashboard — and that is the equivalent of driving at night without headlights.

You Don't Need a Data Team — You Need a System

The businesses I work with in Abuja and across Nigeria are not large corporations. They are cooperatives, logistics companies, clinics, retailers, and NGOs — and most of them were tracking everything in disconnected spreadsheets before we built them a proper system.

A well-built Excel template or Power BI dashboard does not require a data analyst on staff. It requires a one-time setup — and from that point, the business owner opens a single file and sees exactly what is happening.

Start Small — One Dashboard, One Decision

The biggest mistake SME owners make when they decide to "get into data" is trying to track everything at once. Start with the one decision that costs you the most when you get it wrong.

For a retailer, that might be inventory — knowing when to reorder before you run out. For a service business, it might be which clients generate the most revenue per hour of work. For a cooperative, it might be tracking loan repayments and interest distributions accurately.

You do not need to become a data analyst. You need a system that shows you the right numbers at the right time — so you can make the call that moves your business forward.
